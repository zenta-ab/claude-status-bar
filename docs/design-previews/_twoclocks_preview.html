Verdict Ring

The gauge shows where you are. The colour shows who wins the race — depletion or reset — and nothing else. A percentage never sets a colour.

The worked example

weekly ring44 % used · resets Fri 07:00, in 3d 6h
burn 0.0081 %/min → dry in 4d 18h → slack +1d 12h
p_cut 0.03  →  SAFE (teal, no badge)
session disc56 % used · resets in 2h 48m
burn 0.410 %/min → dry in 1h 47m → 61 min short
p_cut 0.94  →  DRY_EARLY k=0.51 (hole + orange–red)

44 % and 56 % are almost the same number, and the icon renders them in opposite colours. That is the whole design. The ring is green not because 44 is a small number but because at that burn rate the week ends before the quota does; the disc is red not because 56 is a big number but because the session quota ends 61 minutes before the window resets. A percentage-driven icon paints both of these the same shade of amber (see the control at the bottom).

Two structural cues, one per window, spatially separated so you can tell which clock is losing: a hole in the middle means this session runs dry early; a badge in the corner means this week does. Both are transparency, not colour, so they survive greyscale, every form of colour blindness, and any taskbar tint.

SPENTP ≥ 99 % or REM < 2qfull 360° red + hole + badgeThe race is over. Exempt from every dwell and rate limit — shown on the first poll.

The gate is deliberately two-dimensional: a verdict needs both a confident probability and a shortfall big enough to matter. p_cut = 0.9 with a three-minute shortfall is TIGHT, not red — being three minutes short is not being dead in the water. Symmetrically, a comfortable probability with a five-minute margin is also TIGHT, not green. Everything the four confident gates decline to claim falls into TIGHT, which is therefore the honest default rather than a middle band.

Every forecaster in the shortlist can feed this: p_cut is a probability the model already computes (EWMA-rate states it directly; the band models get it from where zero sits inside their slack interval), and shortfall_med is the median of that same interval. The state machine never sees a burn rate.

Scaling matters: 60 minutes of shortfall inside a five-hour window is a ruined afternoon; 60 minutes inside a seven-day window is a rounding error. The absolute floors stop the session thresholds collapsing to noise. Worked example: k = (60.6 − 15)/(105 − 15) = 0.51 → render step 0.50.

Hysteresis

Four independent mechanisms, because a tray icon that changes colour twice a minute is worse than no icon. All of them are asymmetric in the same direction: escalate quickly, de-escalate slowly. Warning late costs the user an interrupted refactor; warning too long costs a glance.

Discontinuities

  • Window rollover → MEASURING immediately, verdict history cleared. The old verdict described a window that no longer exists; carrying it forward for three minutes of “de-escalation dwell” would be a lie with a countdown on it.
  • Clock jump, sleep/resume, suspected ceiling change (the +50 % boost expiring on 13 September is exactly this) → hold the current verdict for at most 2 polls, then MEASURING until the forecaster clears its own re-seed period.
  • Never classify on a discontinuity poll. A rollover looks like a huge negative delta and a resume looks like a huge positive one; both must be dropped, not fed to the gate.

Notification, separately from the icon

A toast fires only on the confirmed SAFE|TIGHT → DRY_EARLY transition, at most once per window, re-arming only after a rollover or after 10 minutes back in SAFE. The icon is ambient; the toast is an interruption and must be rarer than the icon it accompanies.

All states

Two palettes, not one compromise colour. The app swaps icon sets on the Windows SystemUsesLightTheme change broadcast, so each palette only has to work against a single background. The light set is the same three hues at roughly 15 % lower lightness.

The two clocks are independent

Ring and disc carry separate verdicts, so “the session is fine but the week is what bites” — the common case for a Max user — is a state the icon can actually express. It is also why the redundant cues are split by position rather than merged into one alert.

Simulated with Víénot linear-RGB matrices applied to the real artwork, not to swatches. The simulation changed the design. An earlier version leaned on an amber→red lightness ramp to separate TIGHT from DRY_EARLY; under deuteranopia the whole warm family collapses into one yellow and that separation simply is not there. So the ramp was demoted to decoration and the structural cues were promoted to carry the state.

What colour actually delivers is a three-way split, and that part survives every simulation:

  • achromatic → MEASURING. Chroma versus no-chroma is the one distinction no form of colour blindness removes, and the slate is pushed to an extreme lightness so it also separates from SAFE by brightness.
  • cool → SAFE. Teal, not green: the blue component survives deuteranopia and protanopia as a distinct lavender-blue, where a true green would land in the same yellow as the warm states.
  • warm → TIGHT, DRY_EARLY, SPENT.

Inside the warm family colour delivers nothing, and the design no longer asks it to. TIGHT and DRY_EARLY are separated entirely by transparency — the centre hole for the session, the corner badge for the week — both punched through to the taskbar and therefore invariant under every colour transform, including full greyscale. That is precisely why the badge fires on the whole of DRY_EARLY rather than on the top of the ramp: making it an intensity marker would have left k = 0.25 indistinguishable from TIGHT for a deuteranope, which is the one confusion this icon cannot afford.

The gap that remains: SPENT versus DRY k=1.0. Both warm, both carrying hole and badge, differing only by a full 360° sweep and a slightly different red — so under deuteranopia at 16 px they are the same icon. That is acceptable: they are adjacent states meaning “about to be cut off” and “cut off”, and the full sweep is genuinely readable at 24 px and up. The k=0.25 to k=1.0 ramp is likewise flat for a deuteranope, and that is deliberate rather than regrettable, because no decision hangs on it.

Control: what percentage-driven colour looks like

Same data, same geometry, colour taken from the raw percentage on a green–amber–red ramp. 44 % and 56 % land two shades apart: both amber, both mildly worrying, neither actionable. Nothing in the left icon tells you that one of those two windows will cut you off in 107 minutes and the other will not. The right icon spends exactly the same pixels and answers the question.

Where this breaks

  • The intensity ramp is decoration. Five values of k render as roughly three distinguishable colours in a 60-pixel disc for normal vision, and as one colour under deuteranopia. It survives only because it is free; every decision the user makes is carried by the four states and the two punches. If it had to justify its pixels it would be cut.
  • A 17-pixel dot is now load-bearing. On the weekly ring, TIGHT and DRY_EARLY differ only by the badge for anyone who cannot use the warm ramp. Lose the badge — an aggressive scaling mode, a taskbar that clips the icon — and that whole distinction goes with it.
  • The badge eats 13 % of the weekly ring. There is no free space in a circle inscribed in a square. If weekly sweep is near 40 % the badge sits right on the arc head, which is the worst place for it.
  • MEASURING is the most dangerous state and it has the least redundancy. It is carried by chroma and lightness only; the hollow badge that would make it structural is unreadable below 24 px. A refusal that reads as a verdict is the worst failure available to this icon, so the mitigation lives outside it: the tooltip leads with the word measuring and the reason, and the state never renders in a verdict hue, not even for one frame during a transition.
  • Two independent verdicts is one more than most users will parse. Ring-red / disc-green requires the user to have learned which ring is which. The panel must teach it; the icon cannot.
  • Everything downstream of p_cut inherits its calibration. The state machine is only as honest as the probability it is handed, and by the forecasters’ own admission that probability is an unvalidated prior until real session logs exist. The 0.20 / 0.80 gates should be treated as ordinal cut-points, not as calibrated risk.
  • Under 20 px on a 100 % DPI display the whole thing is 256 pixels. Nothing more can be added. Any further signal has to live in the flyout, the tooltip, or a toast.
