Pace Wedge

The pace marker is not a line drawn on the gauge — it is the boundary of a re‑coloured sector inside the gauge. The wedge only repaints pixels the locked structure already spends, so the encoding costs zero new pixels at 16 px. The two pixels the brief budgeted are spent instead on a hairline that appears only at 24 px and above, where they are affordable.

Why this is exact, not a heuristic

With p = fraction used and e = fraction of the window elapsed (e = 1 − t_reset/W, both free from a single snapshot):

t_dep = W·e(1−p)/p     t_reset = W(1−e)   →   dead time D = W·(p−e)/p   →   D > 0  ⇔  p > e

“Fill leads tick” is algebraically identical to “you get cut off before reset” under the cumulative-pace estimator — no rate model, no history, no smoothing, correct on the first poll after a cold start. The wedge is the numerator and the whole filled sector is the denominator, so the picture is also calibrated: D = W × wedge / fill. In the worked example 300 × 12/56 = 64 min, matching the brief's hand calculation exactly.

The wedge always spans [min(p,e), max(p,e)] — it is the lead or the lag, and its colour is the sign of the race. The user never has to remember which side of a tick is bad: orange exists ⇔ you will be cut off. There is no green anywhere in the icon, so there is no red/green pair to fail.

States

SAFE · 38% used, 55% elapsed
32 px: ghost wedge + hairline show 17 pp of banked credit — you can spend faster. 16 px: nothing at all. The icon is silent when you are fine; the entire 16 px budget is reserved for the alarm.
TIGHT / ON PACE · 50% used, 49% elapsed
Coincidence is a state, not a failure. At 32 px the hairline lands on the fill edge, half over accent and half over track — a crisp positive mark, not an absence. At 16 px it degrades to “no orange”.
DRY EARLY · 56% used, 44% elapsed → 64 min dead
43.2° hot wedge = 8.0 px² of orange inside a 9.2 px disc at 16 px. Survives. The ring simultaneously shows the week banked — two independent verdicts, no confusion, because they are spatially separated.
NO VERDICT · fresh window, 2% used, 3% elapsed
E < W/30 or P < 3q → the ratio is quantisation noise. Wedge suppressed; 32 px shows a dotted hairline (line known, verdict withheld). 16 px shows nothing — identical to ON PACE. That collision is benign: both states call for the same action, none.
MINIMUM-INK CLAMP · 52% used, 48% elapsed (4 pp, ~24 min dead)
True wedge 14.4° → ~2.6 px², invisible. At 16 px only, a real gap of ≥3 pp is drawn at a floor of 18° and 65% alpha. The clamp extends the wedge inward, so the fill edge — and therefore the raw percentage — stays truthful; only the internal boundary lies, and only in the warn-earlier direction.

The hairline deliberately stops at the disc rim and never crosses the transparent gap: every pixel of it sits on a known opaque background, so one ink colour works on both taskbars. A nub poking into the gap would have to contrast with #202020 and #f3f3f3 at once, and at 16 px it would close the 0.9 px gap and weld the disc to the ring.

The 16 px floor lands at roughly 40–50 minutes of projected dead time. That is the encoding's best defence rather than something to hide: a 20-minute shortfall is below the threshold at which a user can usefully change behaviour anyway. The pixel floor and the actionability floor are approximately the same number. The minimum-ink clamp buys back the 3–8 pp band at the cost of area fidelity in exactly the range where area fidelity stops mattering.

Where it breaks

1. Light taskbar, ahead of pace. The hot wedge is the outermost part of the fill, so on #f3f3f3 the fill edge becomes orange (L* 63) against a light track (L* ~83) instead of blue (L* 47) against it. Reading the raw percentage gets ~10 L* harder precisely when you are overspending. The pace boundary itself stays crisp (ΔL 16 plus a full hue flip), so the verdict never degrades — only the magnitude read does.

2. 16 px cannot separate ON PACE from NO VERDICT. Both are “no orange”. Benign, because both mean “do nothing”, but real — only the tooltip disambiguates.

3. Two overspends at once put orange on ring and disc together. Still legible, but the icon is then ~20% orange, and the severity gradient between “week is 3 h short” and “week is 40 h short” is carried by arc length alone, which at 16 px resolves to about three steps.

4. e must come from the reported countdown, never from local-clock arithmetic on an absolute reset timestamp. A DST shift or a laptop resume would otherwise move the tick by an hour with no other symptom. Suppress the wedge for one poll after any monotonic-clock discontinuity.

5. A moving ceiling breaks it silently. The +50% boost expiring on 13 Sept rescales p without touching e; the wedge jumps and looks like a burn spike. Treat a detected ceiling change as a rollover: suppress, re-seed.

6. It is a pace comparison, not a forecast. It assumes the rest of the window resembles the window so far. Twenty idle minutes do not shrink the wedge, they only stop it growing. The EWMA and Theil–Sen recency estimators are precisely what disagree with it, and that disagreement belongs in the panel, not the icon.
